Questions for you

Each one: what we saw, why it's a problem, and what we need from you.

1. Slippage: keep Trails' Auto, or force 0.5%?

What we saw. We pass slippageTolerance={0.005} to the Pay widget, but the QuoteIntent request it sends to Trails has no slippageTolerance at all, and its "Transaction details" shows no "Max slippage" row. In 0xtrails 0.20.0 (the latest), the Pay screen's quote call passes every option except this one. Without it the widget reads its own saved setting (localStorage['trails-slippage-tolerance']), which is empty for every buyer, so Trails falls back to Auto.

Why it matters. Your point 4 asked for 0.5% so buyers aren't charged more than expected when the price moves. Right now that limit isn't applied: every purchase so far, the "5%" ones and today's "0.5%" one, ran on Trails' Auto, and we don't control or know the limit Auto picks. Seen 12 Nov: on the Base USDC purchase, Trails' own record of the intent shows Auto chose maxSlippage 0.1, i.e. 10%, against the 0.5% you asked for (it cost nothing on that quote, which fixed the exact amount paid, but it is the limit in force). The widget also shows a red "Price impact 25.73%" on a $0.10 order: that's the $0.03 fees against the price, not a price move, but it can alarm a buyer on cheap listings.

We need you to choose. (a) Keep Auto and report the ignored prop to Trails: no code change. (b) Force 0.5% by writing '0.005' into that localStorage key just before the widget opens; it works today, but depends on the widget's internals (a future update could silently undo it) and overrides a buyer's own slippage choice if the widget lets them set one.

2. Is the Trails rate limit per key?

What we saw. During testing Trails answered our status checks with 429 Quota rate limit exceeded. One tester, a couple of open purchases, and the widget's own calls were enough to hit it.

Why it matters. Status checks run in each buyer's browser, and every browser uses the same public key. If the limit is per key, a busy day with many buyers at once could hit it in production: checks would slow down and "is yours" would arrive late. We added a back off (30 s, 60 s, 2 min), so nothing breaks, but it would be slow.

We need to know the limit on our key and whether it's per key or per browser. If per key, moving status checks to the backend checkout source or asking Trails for a higher limit becomes more urgent.

3. Start checking status at the first quote, or when the buyer presses Pay?

What we saw. As soon as the widget shows a price it creates a quote, and we start checking that quote's status with Trails (GetIntent, every 3 s, then 15 s). If the buyer only looks at the price and presses Back, we keep checking for up to 15 minutes for a purchase that never happens.

Why we do it this way. Your doc saves the purchase at onQuote but doesn't say which event starts the checks. We first started at onOriginConfirmation ("payment landed"), but on the first real purchase (10 Nov) that event never fired, because Trails recorded the deposit with a placeholder hash. The buyer paid and the site would have said nothing. Starting at the quote removes any dependence on widget events.

Why it's a problem. Wasted requests on our shared key (see question 2) for buyers who never pay, which is most widget opens.

Proposal. Start at onSign (the buyer pressed Pay), keeping "payment landed" and "success" as backups. Opening the widget and going Back would then cost nothing. The risk: we haven't confirmed that onSign fires for every wallet; if it and both backups failed, a paid purchase would go unwatched. We need your OK to switch (we'd verify on one paid run), or to keep today's safer behaviour.

4. Widget problems to raise with Trails

None of these come from our code and we can't fix them on our side; they're from inside the 0xtrails package.

  1. Quoted again in USDT right after Pay (seen over WalletConnect). The widget switched the token to USDT and made a new quote 5 seconds after the buyer pressed Pay with POL. Problem: it confused tracking (we fixed our side) and could confuse a buyer who sees a different token. Didn't happen on the MetaMask extension.
  2. Its status screen stayed on "Swap" ("taking longer than expected") after the purchase had already succeeded. Problem: the buyer thinks it's stuck; our drawer now covers this by moving on, but only once our own check sees the result.
  3. Console errors and warnings: Maximum update depth exceeded in its Pay screen, Quote disabled … malformed "0.1", and its own GetIntentReceipt 400s before the buyer pays. Problem: no visible breakage, but noise in every buyer's console and a possible performance cost.

We need you to pass these to Trails, or tell us who should.

5. Can an ABORTED intent ever have taken the buyer's money?

What we saw. A payment cancelled in the wallet ended at Trails as INVALID ("deposit transaction FAILED", origin ABORTED). We now treat FAILED, ABORTED or INVALID as "never paid" when the deposit didn't succeed and there's no origin transaction hash, and end it quietly with no message.

Why it matters. If Trails can mark an intent ABORTED after the buyer's money already moved, our rule would hide a real problem: the buyer would see nothing, not even "payment didn't go through".

We need to know whether that case exists. If it does, we'll show a message for ABORTED instead of ending quietly.

6. "Is yours" when our sale record never arrives

What the code does. Your rule 1: only our own sale record (the poller's SALE row with this buyer) may show "is yours"; until then the drawer says "Purchase confirmed. Preparing your receipt...". We follow that. But we added a fallback: if Trails says SUCCEEDED and our record still hasn't shown the buyer after 20 minutes (for example the poller is down or far behind), the drawer or the toast shows "is yours" anyway, based on Trails alone.

Why we added it. Otherwise the buyer waits 20 minutes on "Preparing your receipt..." and then gets no answer at all, though our calls always name the buyer as recipient, so a succeeded Trails transaction means the domain went to them.

Why it's a problem. It breaks rule 1, and that screen's View Receipt opens /receipt/:orderHash, which reads the same missing record, so the buyer would see an empty receipt.

We need you to choose. (a) Keep it. (b) Our recommendation: after 20 minutes show "Purchase confirmed. Your receipt is taking longer than usual; the domain will appear in your account shortly." with a Polygonscan link instead of View Receipt. Small change.

7. A screen for a cancelled signature?

What we saw. Your definition of done lists a "cancelled signature" screen. When the buyer rejects in the wallet, the widget itself shows "Try again" on the same quote, and we draw nothing of our own (onSignReject is wired through but the drawer doesn't use it).

Why it matters. Only whether the checklist item counts as done; the buyer isn't stuck, since "Try again" and Back both work.

We need to know whether the widget's own "Try again" is enough, or you want our own screen (for example "Payment cancelled in your wallet. Nothing was charged." with Try again and Back).

8. Paying directly and through Trails at the same time (new, 12 Nov)

What we saw. In the lost race test the same wallet bought directly with USDT in one tab and paid through Trails in another. The direct buy won; Trails refunded 0.05 USDT on Polygon (intent 0x0d937952…3e01, REFUNDED). The Trails tab showed "is yours", not "Payment refunded", because your rule 1 (a sale record naming this buyer means done) is checked before Trails' status, and the record named this same buyer.

Why it matters. "Is yours" is true (the domain is theirs), but the buyer is never told that their second payment was refunded, as USDT, minus Trails' fees (about 0.03 USD here). Rare, and nothing is lost. Against a different buyer the drawer goes to Refunded as designed.

We need to know whether this is fine as is, or whether "is yours" should add a line when Trails also says REFUNDED for this purchase.

9. The receipt says "You paid 0.10 USDT" for a Trails purchase (new, 12 Nov)

What we saw. The receipt page for weiwuxianlanwangji.og (bought through Trails with about 1.29 POL) says "You paid 0.10 USDT". The receipt reads our sale record, which only knows the order price, not how the buyer paid. Same on the drawer's "is yours" screen. Separately, and older than Trails: "Seller received" and "Marketplace fee" are rounded to two decimals, so a 0.10 sale shows 0.10 received and a 0.00 fee next to "2.5% was deducted".

Why it matters. A Trails buyer sees a different amount and token from what left their wallet.

We need to know whether to leave it until the backend's paymentMethod on sales (your backend checkout source), or change the label now (for example "Price 0.10 USDT").
