Trails checkout · how it all works

Pay with any token, end to end

One place that explains the whole "Any token, any chain" purchase: who takes part, what a checkout, quote and intent are, where the money goes, how it worked when the browser did everything (commit 1622027), and how it works now that our backend does the checking and tracking (02cd1d9 plus the 18 Nov review fixes). 1. The idea in one paragraph

Every listing on our marketplace is a signed Seaport order that can only be paid in USDT on Polygon. Many buyers don't have that. "Any token, any chain" lets them pay with whatever they hold (USDC on Base, BNB on BNB Chain, ETH, and so on). Trails takes their token, converts and moves it to USDT on Polygon, and then, on the buyer's behalf, runs the two Polygon transactions that buy the domain: approve USDT and fill the Seaport order, with the domain sent to the buyer's wallet. If the purchase can't happen (someone bought it first, the listing was cancelled), Trails sends the USDT back to the buyer on Polygon instead. That's a refund.

Our job is to: decide who may use it, prepare exactly those two transactions, show Trails' payment widget, follow the purchase until it ends, and tell the buyer the result, even if they closed the drawer, changed page, reloaded or opened another device.

2. Who takes part

BuyerTheir wallet (MetaMask, or a phone wallet over WalletConnect) holds some token on some chain.
Our frontendThe buy drawer (BuyFlowModal), the Trails widget inside it, and an app wide tracker that follows purchases.
TrailsA payment service. Gives a price (quote), takes the payment, converts and bridges it (through Relay), runs our calls on Polygon.
Our backendSince phases 1 to 6: checks the listing, builds the calls, verifies Trails' quote, stores and tracks every checkout, sends emails.
PolygonWhere the listing lives: Seaport (the order contract), USDT, and the domain NFT contract.

3. The words, in plain language

Listing / order
The seller's signed Seaport order: "I give this domain for X USDT". Identified by its orderHash (0x plus 64 hex characters).
Checkout
Our record of one attempt to buy one listing through Trails. Has a checkoutId. Lives about 15 minutes if never paid. Before: invented in the browser (crypto.randomUUID()) and kept in localStorage. Now: created and stored by our backend (a database row).
Prepared checkout
What "prepare" returns: checkout id, buyer, price (destination: chain 137, USDT, amount in minor units), expiresAt, the two calls, and the route settings for the widget.
Calls (destination calls)
The two Polygon transactions Trails must run for us: 1) USDT approve to Seaport, 2) Seaport fulfillAdvancedOrder with the recipient set to the buyer. We hand them to the widget unchanged.
Quote
Trails' price for "pay with this token on this chain": how much of the buyer's token, fees included. Each time the buyer picks another token, or after a while, the widget makes a new quote.
Intent (intentId)
Trails' own record of one quote and, once paid, of the whole payment: deposit, bridge, our calls, refund. One checkout can see several intents (one per quote); only the paid one matters. The widget tells us each new one through onQuote.
Attach
Linking an intent to our checkout ("this quote belongs to that purchase"). Before: saved in localStorage. Now: sent to the backend (API 3), which first checks the intent really pays this listing to this buyer.
Deposit / origin chain
The buyer's payment on their own chain (for example Base). That's the moment money leaves their wallet.
Destination chain
Always Polygon here: where the USDT arrives and our two calls run.
Origin calls vs destination calls
Where Trails puts our calls inside the intent. Normally under destinationCalls (run on Polygon after the bridge). When the buyer already pays with USDT on Polygon, there's nothing to bridge, and Trails puts them under originCalls. That difference is bug F9.
Route
Widget settings: bridge provider RELAY, no fallback, slippage 0.005. Now sent by the backend. (The widget currently ignores slippage, F3.)
Sale record
Our marketplace's own record that the order was filled, written by our indexer after it sees the Polygon transaction. "Done" means our sale record shows this buyer bought it.
Refund
When our calls can't run, Trails sends the USDT (not the original token) back to the buyer's wallet on Polygon.
Verified wallet
The buyer signed a free message proving they own the wallet; the login token then carries that wallet. The backend needs it before prepare and attach. Valid 60 minutes (we refresh at 55).
USDT minor units
USDT has 6 decimals, so amounts are strings like "100000" = 0.10 USDT.

4. Where the money actually goes

The same in both versions. This is Trails' part; we only prepare it and watch it.

1

Buyer

Presses Pay in the widget. Their wallet asks to approve and send, for example, 0.14 USDC on Base (price plus Trails' fee). Sometimes it asks twice (allow the token, then send). The first "yes" can already move the money.

2

Trails

Receives the deposit on Base, converts and bridges it through Relay, and ends with exactly the listing price in USDT on Polygon, in a wallet Trails controls for this intent.

3

Polygon

Trails runs our two calls from that wallet: approve USDT to Seaport, then fill the order. Seaport pays the seller and sends the domain NFT straight to the buyer. One transaction: the destinationTxHash.

4a

Success

Our indexer sees the sale and writes the sale record (30 to 60 seconds later). The domain shows in My Domains within 10 to 15 minutes.

4b

Or a refund

If the fill fails (sold, cancelled, expired, changed), the USDT goes back to the buyer on Polygon: the refundTxHash. Not converted back to USDC.

5. Before: the browser did everything (up to 1622027)

Still in the code as a rollback: NEXT_PUBLIC_TRAILS_SOURCE=browser. Removed in phase 9 once production is stable.

1

Show the option

"Any token" appeared when our env switch was on, the key was set, and the buyer's wallet was in the pilot wallet list in .env (NEXT_PUBLIC_TRAILS_PILOT_WALLETS).

2

Prepare, in the browser

On Continue, the browser loaded the order from our API, then read Polygon directly (orderChecks.service.ts): not cancelled or filled, seller still owns the domain and approved Seaport, counter unchanged, not expiring soon. Then it built the two calls itself (buildDestinationCalls.ts) and invented a checkout id.

3

Widget

Opened with the calls and a hardcoded slippage. Each quote (onQuote) was saved to localStorage with the domain name and price (checkoutStore.service.ts), keeping the last 3 earlier intents in case an older quote was the one paid.

4

Follow it

Every 3 seconds the browser asked Trails directly (GetIntent, then GetIntentReceipt once paid, in trailsReceipt.service.ts) and our sale record (GET /marketplacev2/orders/transactions/:orderHash), and mixed them into one status (intentStatus.ts). Rule 1: if our sale record shows this buyer, it's done.

5

After a reload

The tracker read localStorage and resumed anything paid in the last 24 hours. Another device or another browser knew nothing.

Why that wasn't enough for production. Anyone could edit the browser code and send different calls (the server never checked them). The pilot list was in the frontend bundle. If the buyer closed the tab, nobody followed the purchase, so no refund email. Every buyer's browser polled Trails (rate limits, our key in every browser's requests). Only the browser that paid knew the purchase existed.

6. Now: our backend checks and tracks (02cd1d9 + review fixes)

The default. Six backend APIs under /api/v1/marketplacev2/trails; numbers below match the backend team's document. All go through callBackend in trailsBackend.service.ts, the only file that calls them.

1

API 1 · GET /config, when the drawer opens

The server answers enabled, available (may this user use Trails), minPriceUsdt (0.10), maxPriceUsdt and the route. "Any token" shows only if our env switch and key are on, the server says available, and the price is in range (checkoutGate.ts). Until it answers, or if it fails, the option stays hidden.

2

Verify the wallet

On Continue, ensureWalletVerified runs. If the login has no fresh verification for this wallet, the drawer shows "Verify your wallet" and the buyer signs a free message. Rejecting it returns to the payment options.

3

API 2 · POST /checkouts (prepare)

We send only the orderHash. The server takes the buyer from the login, does all the live listing checks, builds the two calls, creates the checkout row (15 minute window) and returns the prepared checkout. It also holds the listing for 2 minutes against other Trails buyers (CHECKOUT_IN_PROGRESS). We double check the server's buyer is the connected wallet, otherwise we stop with the "wrong wallet" screen. Errors map to screens (own listing, expiring soon, price, unavailable, someone else paying...).

4

Widget

Opens with the server's calls and route. The buyer picks a token; Trails makes a quote and tells us its intentId (onQuote).

5

API 3 · POST /checkouts/:id/intent (attach), on every quote

The server fetches that intent from Trails and checks it (about eight checks: still in time, right chain and amount, exactly our three calls under destinationCalls, buyer and leftover go to the buyer, a real route...). It answers HTTP 200 with a status: attached (fine, carry on), mismatch (refused: we close the widget, "Couldn't start the payment, nothing was charged"), or expired (the 15 minutes passed: "Your price hold ran out", Start again). A refused later quote doesn't spoil an earlier good one. ALREADY_ATTACHED (409) means the current quote is already paid: we just log it.

6

Pay

The widget's onSign fires. From now on the drawer remembers "Pay was pressed" and ignores any late refusal from step 5, because money may be moving (review R1). The tracker starts following the checkout if it isn't already.

7

API 4 · GET /checkouts/:id, every 5 s (15 s after 5 minutes)

The server refreshes from Trails when its data is older than 5 seconds and from our sale record, and answers one combined state: status, intentStatus, destinationTxHash, refundTxHash, sale, refund. Once paid, the drawer swaps the widget for "Payment in progress". When it ends: "X is yours", "Payment refunded" or "Payment didn't go through". The browser no longer calls Trails for status at all.

8

In the background, without us

The server keeps tracking every paid checkout even if every tab is closed (backend Sprint 12) and sends the buyer the purchase or refund email (Sprint 14).

9

API 5 · GET /checkouts?status=&limit=

The buyer's own checkouts, newest first. Used after a load or a login to pick up running purchases and refunds (section 9), and every 15 s by the /history "Purchase in progress" banner (executing and failed).

10

API 6 · sale receipt (later)

For a Trails sale the receipt will say "Network fee: included in the Trails fee" instead of gas figures. Ships after the backend's production release (our phase 7).

9. After the drawer: the app wide tracker

The drawer doesn't own the watching. One tracker for the whole tab (core/services/trails/checkoutTracker.ts, polling in watchCheckout.ts) follows every purchase; the drawer only listens to it. So:

  • Drawer closed, Back pressed, or another page: the watch carries on. When it ends and nothing on screen shows it, a toast appears ("X is yours", "Payment refunded", "Payment didn't go through"). The toast is drawn by TrailsPurchaseWatcher in pages/_app.tsx.
  • Reload: the watcher asks the list API for the buyer's running purchases and refunds of the last day and watches them again. A result is shown once per browser (a small "seen" list in localStorage).
  • Another device: same list API, so a running purchase or a refund shows there too. A finished success doesn't pop up again on a new device (R2), and a failed one only while it can still turn into a refund.
  • Logging in later: the list is read again on every login, not only on load (R3).
  • Login expired during a purchase: the status call gets 401. We never treat that as an end: the drawer says "Log in again to see your purchase status. Your purchase keeps going in the meantime." (or one toast if closed), we retry every 15 s, and carry on after the login.
  • Server busy: a rate limit (429) waits 30 s, 60 s, then 2 minutes; other dropped requests just retry.

10. Safety rules and why they exist

The server builds the calls

So nobody can change the browser code to buy something else or send the domain elsewhere. The verifier then checks Trails' intent contains exactly those calls.

Buyer from the login

The server ignores any buyer we send. We additionally stop if the login's wallet isn't the connected one, so the domain can't go to a different wallet than the one paying.

Pay pressed is never undone

Once Pay is pressed we never say "nothing was charged", even if the buyer rejects a second wallet request: the first one may already have moved the money (seen in T5).

Unpaid quotes stay quiet

A buyer who looks at a price and leaves never gets a scary message, and never sees a "purchase in progress" banner.

Success needs our sale record

"X is yours" waits until our own sale record shows this buyer, not just Trails saying succeeded.

Fail closed

If /config fails or hasn't answered, the option is hidden. Our env switch can turn it off whatever the server says.

13. What Mohit's code review found (18 Nov) and what changed

Review of 02cd1d9. All three are fixed on the frontend (uncommitted). Details: progress doc, "Code review by Mohit".

R1

The drawer could say "Nothing was charged" after the buyer had paid. On every quote we ask the server (attach) whether the quote is fine. If the answer was "mismatch" or "expired", the drawer closed the widget and said nothing was charged. But that answer can arrive a second or two after the buyer pressed Pay, so the buyer would read "nothing was charged" while their money was moving. Second half: a checkout that ended as mismatch was always closed silently, even if paid. Third half (backend): when the first quote fails the check, the server marks the checkout mismatch and doesn't save that intent, so a paid one is never tracked and gets no refund email.

Why it matters to us: it needs the server to wrongly refuse a good quote, which is exactly what F9 does today with Polygon USDT.

Fix: once Pay is pressed (widget onSign) the drawer ignores late refusals and keeps following the purchase. A refused checkout is silent only if nothing was paid; if a payment shows, we keep watching until the real result (success or refund), as the backend developer asked on 18 Nov. Pay pressed with no payment showing yet is watched 15 minutes, then "Payment didn't go through… Trails refunds it". The Pay flag is never undone by a rejected wallet request. Backend half: being fixed in their Sprint 21 (keep tracking a paid refused intent and send the normal emails).

R1 in easy words

Think of it as two messages that can arrive in the wrong order.

How it works: the widget shows a price (say 0.14 USDC). In the background, the drawer asks our server "is this price okay?". The server answers "okay" or "doesn't match". On "doesn't match" the drawer closes the widget and says "Couldn't start the payment. Nothing was charged." That's correct, if the buyer hasn't paid yet.

The problem is timing. The server's answer takes a second or two. A fast buyer may already have pressed Pay and approved in their wallet:

TimeWhat happens
0 sThe widget shows the price; the drawer asks the server "is this okay?"
1 sThe buyer presses Pay and approves. Money leaves their wallet.
2 sThe server's answer arrives: "doesn't match"
2 sThe drawer says "Nothing was charged". Wrong: they just paid.

Then it gets worse. Our frontend closed that purchase silently, so no "payment failed" message ever came. Our server never saved that refused price, so it doesn't follow the payment either, and if Trails refunds it, no refund email goes out. The buyer paid, was told they didn't, and nobody follows up.

Can it really happen? Only if the server wrongly refuses a good price. Mohit says that should never happen, but F9 shows it does: today the server refuses every good Polygon USDT price.

What we changed (frontend):

  1. Once Pay is pressed, the drawer ignores a late "doesn't match": the widget stays and we keep following the purchase.
  2. "Doesn't match" is silent only if nothing was paid. If money moved, we keep watching until the real result and show it: "is yours" or "Payment refunded" (backend developer's request, 18 Nov). Only if no result shows in time does the buyer see "Payment didn't go through. If money left your wallet, Trails refunds it."
  3. Cancelling a second wallet popup doesn't undo it: in T5 the first approval had already sent the money, so once Pay is pressed we always assume money may have moved.

What Mohit's team must change (backend): if Trails reports money arrived for a price the server refused, keep following it and raise an alert, so the buyer still gets the refund email. The backend developer confirmed on 18 Nov this is being done in Sprint 21, after which both sides agree on the final status.

R2

A new device showed old results again. After a load we toasted every finished purchase of the last 24 hours that this browser hadn't shown. "Already shown" is remembered per browser, so logging in on a new phone popped up "X is yours" for purchases the buyer had already seen on the laptop.

Fix: after a load we only pick up running purchases and refunds (his suggestion). A failed one only while it can still become a refund (20 minutes). A refund can still show once per device, on purpose: it's money they must know about.

R3

Logging in later didn't pick up running purchases. We read the purchase list once, when the app first loaded, and only if the buyer was already logged in. Logging in afterwards (same session, no reload) missed them.

Fix: the list is read on load and again on every login, using the same login signal the rest of the app uses. The code moved into TrailsPurchaseWatcher in pages/_app.tsx, inside the login provider, so it can see logins.

Earlier, on 11 Nov, Mohit reviewed the browser path and raised six points, all fixed then (commit 1622027): the Trails code loaded on every page (now loaded only when needed), tracking tied to the drawer (now the app wide tracker), refund wording, slippage, unpaid "agreed" quotes polled forever (now 15 minutes), and widget console logs. Write up: trails-mohit-review.html.
